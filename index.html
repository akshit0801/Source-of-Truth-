FAQs

What exactly is SKAI Space?
A future-skills space in your school

Kits, missions, an AI companion and dashboards, where students learn AI, robotics, electronics and engineering by building real things.

Which grades is it for?
Grades 1 to 10

Missions scale with age: younger students start with guided builds; older students take on open-ended challenges.

Do we change our timetable?
No. One period a week per section

Missions run in a regular period and map to CBSE's Composite Skills Lab guidelines and NEP 2020.

Who runs the sessions?
Your teachers, with Swiftee

Teachers are trained in a one-day workshop. Swiftee guides students through hints, testing and reflection.

Does it meet the CBSE skill lab requirement?
Yes, built for the Composite Skills Lab

It runs in your existing room, includes teacher training and produces the evidence CBSE asks for.

How do we see progress?
Dashboard, reports, Skills Passport

A usage report each term, a year-end impact report, and a QR-linked Skills Passport for parents.

Who will we contact in the school?
Your CG Account Manager

The CG Account Manager supports the school throughout the year, in person and on call.

How will the school replace damaged materials?
Raise it with the CG Account Manager

A ticket is raised and the material is replaced within the agreed replacement SLAs.

Who looks after the lab day to day?
Your lab POC

The school's lab POC (not a teacher) opens the lab, keeps devices charged and logs issues with the CG Account Manager.

Curriculum design · How a mission works

Every mission runs the same cycle

This is what makes it transferable: the skill repeats, the domain changes.

1Research & Understand

Meet a real situation and ask the right questions.

2Make

Build something real with hands and mind; test it.

3Innovate

Push past the first solution: improve and adapt it.

4Reflect

Name what worked and why, so the skill transfers.

Digital-first, individual → Maker, in a groupA student first explores and understands a concept on screen, alone. Then students move to a hands-on build as a team, applying what they understood. Understanding always comes before building; that's what turns a build into learning, not assembly.
Evidence 1
Digital evidence

Observed, selected, predicted, sequenced.

Evidence 2
Kit evidence

Made, tested, improved, physically.

Evidence 3
Reflection evidence

Explained the process and the transfer.

Evidence 4
Facilitator evidence

Confirmed collaboration, safety, communication.

✓

Only when all four are complete does a mission become skill progress, not just an activity log. The Skills Passport turns that progress into something a parent can hold and see.
